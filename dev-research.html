    Headless HEC-HMS forecasting pipeline

    Running a basin model for every new forecast by hand is slow and error-prone. This pipeline runs the whole chain without opening the HEC-HMS interface.

    1. Forecast inWRF NetCDF rainfall
    2. Grid & clipVortex → gridded DSS over the basin
    3. InjectParameters & initial conditions set in code
    4. OperateRule-based reservoir releases
    5. RunHeadless HEC-HMS compute
    6. ReportHydrographs, peaks, storage

    What it does

    • Extracts the basin's rainfall from the forecast grid and writes it to HEC-DSS automatically.
    • Injects loss, transform and routing parameters from configuration instead of manual editing.
    • Applies rule-based reservoir operation so releases follow defined operating rules.
    • Runs HEC-HMS headlessly through its scripting interface and collects the results.

    Where it's going

    The same engine is being generalised to several urban and reservoir-regulated basin models behind one common dashboard: pick a model, a forecast file and a basin boundary, run, and see inputs and outputs side by side. Alongside it, I'm rebuilding basin parameterisation (curve number, lag, routing) from first principles with proper calibration and validation.

    Dam-break Population-at-Risk (PAR) dashboard

    A dam-break study isn't finished when the flood map is — the question is who is in its path. This dashboard takes HEC-RAS dam-break results for a dam and turns them into a population-at-risk assessment.

    What it does

    • Loads the dam-break flood extent and depth results from HEC-RAS.
    • Overlays settlements and population to estimate how many people are inside the flooded area.
    • Summarises results per settlement as tables and maps ready for reports.
    • Makes the analysis repeatable for every new dam in a study — same steps, same outputs.

    Why it matters

    Population at risk drives dam hazard classification and emergency action planning. Automating it turns a slow manual GIS exercise into something consistent across many dams.

    Dam-break map series tool for QGIS

    An emergency action plan needs the flood mapped sheet by sheet at a fixed scale, from the dam to where the flood ends. Laying out those sheets by hand takes hours for every dam and every map type. This QGIS tool does it in about a minute.

    1. ReadDam details from the report file
    2. TraceFlood path downstream from the dam
    3. Lay sheetsFixed-scale sheets, overlapping, until the flood ends
    4. Fill layoutTitle, heading, legend, map information
    5. AtlasMap 01, 02, 03 … in QGIS
    6. ExportPNG at any dpi

    What it does

    • Follows the flood along its flow path from the dam and places sheets one after another until every flooded cell is mapped — Sheet 1 automatically fits the dam and its reservoir.
    • Works for any result layer: maximum depth, velocity, hazard, vulnerability, arrival time. You choose the layer and its symbology, the map heading and the legend title.
    • Reuses one sheet index for every theme, so sheet 05 covers the same ground in the depth, velocity and hazard series.
    • Any scale and resolution: the scale text, scale bar and lat/long grid follow the chosen scale.
    • Fills river, basin, peak breach discharge, population at risk and date from a report file, so the map text always matches the analysis.

    Why it matters

    Map preparation is often the slowest step after the modelling. Automating it makes the maps consistent across every dam and every theme, and frees time for checking the results instead of moving rectangles.

    GEE Scout — dataset discovery for any area

    Every new study starts with the same question: which satellite and climate datasets cover my area, at what resolution and for which years? GEE Scout answers it in one step.

    • Takes an area of interest and the project's needs (e.g. rainfall, land cover, terrain, soil moisture).
    • Gathers matching Earth Engine datasets with coverage, resolution and time span.
    • Leaves the final choice to the analyst, with everything needed to decide side by side.
